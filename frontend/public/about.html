About this demo

Heat-Health Early Warning Platform · Smart India Hackathon 2026 · Problem Statement 26083 · Pilot city: Ahmedabad (48 wards)

This is a working prototype. It turns weather forecasts into ward-level heat-health risk, early warnings and concrete actions for Ahmedabad, with a human officer approving every alert. The demo replays the real May 2024 heatwave exactly as the system would have seen it. All scores are model estimates, not clinical predictions.

Try it: guided demo (about 5 minutes)

  1. Early warning, four days ahead. In the day bar at the bottom, click Thu 16, then set Map layer → Chance of Red. Forecasts from three weather models (ECMWF, GFS, ICON) already give Baherampura, Danilimda, Asarwa and Shahpur a 67% chance of Red. IMD's red alert came on 20 May.
  2. The first Red ward. Click Fri 17, set the layer back to Mortality risk (MRI), and click Baherampura on the map. “Why this score” shows how many points each factor adds (heat stress, hot nights, sheet roofs, slum share, distance to care), each labelled live, census or estimate.
  3. What to do about it. Scroll down the ward panel. Department actions from the Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan, safe outdoor work hours (moderate work not advised 9 am–3 pm), and public advisories in English, Hindi and Gujarati.
  4. Indoor heat. Click Thu 23, open All wards and click New Wadaj. Metal and asbestos sheet roofs add almost 6 points on a hot night (roof shares estimated from Census 2011).
  5. Cooling deserts. Set Map layer → Cooling access gap. 8 wards where most people can't walk to a cooling place within 15 minutes, with numbered sites proposed for new cooling centres.
  6. Alerts need a human. Open the Alerts tab and the 2024-05-17 · Red · Draft alert. Messages in three languages, length checks, and an international CAP alert file. Approving needs the team's password, so visitors can look but not send.
  7. Residents on WhatsApp. Back in Alerts, open Resident WhatsApp preview and type Baherampura, then Vatva gujarati. A resident sends a ward name and gets the officer-approved alert in their language. (Simulated here; nothing is sent.)
  8. Health workers catch what the model misses. With Thu 23 selected, open the Reports tab. Vatva reports 6 cases where 0.2 were expected, in a ward the model ranks only 17th. Reports are stored as counts only, with no names or phone numbers. Demo reports are synthetic.
  9. Plan for next summer. Click Baherampura, open What-if, set cool roofs to 50% and click Run scenario. The whole heatwave is re-scored in about a second: 3 Red days avoided with 10 points more tree cover and cool roofs (a scenario estimate).
  10. Evidence and honesty. Click Report card in the grey status bar, then open the Method tab. 4 of 5 IMD red-alert days caught, 4 days' warning, 11 of 11 dangerous days caught with none missed; every formula, data source and limitation on screen.

Where it runs and why: Render

The site runs on Render's free plan as a single Docker container, rebuilt automatically from GitHub on every change. We chose Render because it was the only option that gave us, all at once:

  • a permanent public link (not one that changes every time a laptop restarts);
  • no payment card;
  • support for a server that runs continuously (Python API, database and an hourly forecast job), not just static pages;
  • automatic updates from GitHub.

Problems we faced, and what we did

ProblemWhat we did
The live forecast is sometimes refused. The free weather service (Open-Meteo) limits requests per server address, and Render's free servers share addresses with many other apps. The app retries every hour. When live data is missing, it switches to the May 2024 replay and says so. The replay's weather is stored with the app, so the demo never depends on that service.
A very small server: 512 MB of memory and a small share of one CPU. We measured the app at about 200–300 MB, built the May 2024 demo into the container so it starts in seconds, and switched off the 122-run live probability forecast (the replay still shows its probabilities).
The site sleeps after 15 minutes without visitors. The first visit wakes it in about a minute. A free uptime monitor keeps it awake.
Changes don't persist: the free plan has no permanent disk. Approvals, reports and saved scenarios reset on restart. That's fine for a demo: it always comes back fresh, with the 17 May Red alert ready as a draft.
Sending real SMS / WhatsApp needs a registered business sender (DLT / Meta approval) or a paid account. Delivery is simulated on this site. A WhatsApp reply bot and SMS/voice delivery are built and tested, ready for a registered sender.

A real deployment for the city would run on a government or paid server with a registered SMS/WhatsApp sender; all of these limits then go away, and the code doesn't change.

Good to know

  • Synthetic Health-worker reports in the demo are synthetic, and labelled as such everywhere.
  • Simulated No alert is sent to a real phone from this site.
  • Estimate Sheet-roof and under-5 shares are census-based estimates. Elderly and outdoor-worker shares are held at the city average because no ward-level data exists.
  • Validated on the May 2024 Ahmedabad heatwave (see the report card and the Method tab).
